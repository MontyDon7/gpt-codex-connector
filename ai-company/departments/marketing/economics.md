## 4. Assumptions

1. Budget lines and people cost are fitted to COMPANY.md §5.4; the split into lines, unit costs and role bases is a derivation by this department, not a company fact.
2. Role bases are held at their first-year level across FY2026 to FY2028 (no merit uplift); the pay-review and true-up reserve absorbs the rounding and a minor cost-of-living adjustment. Annual merit above this would need a headcount-timing offset.
3. Recruitment fees (typically 15% to 20% of base for agency-sourced hires) are paid from the `people` department budget, not marketing.
4. Marketing-sourced share of leads: 55% / 60% / 65%. The remainder comes from Sales outbound, client referrals and partner introductions. Win rate, lead qualification and opportunity-to-proposal rates are the company-wide rates (COMPANY.md §5.3, §8) applied equally to marketing-sourced and other leads, except by channel in section 3.2.
5. ACV used for pipeline value equals the company ACV (COMPANY.md §5.1). Marketing-sourced deals may skew smaller (Spark, Pilot) at first; this is covered in the sensitivities.
6. Agent cost in the marketing budget is direct inference and tool seats only (about £6.7k to £8.1k per agent-year). The COMPANY.md §5.2 average of £20k to £35k per agent-year is fully loaded and includes shared platform, governance and oversight time carried by `data`, `it_security` and `product`.
7. Unit costs of AI compute fall about 15% per year (COMPANY.md §8 item 4); the AI line is sized after that decline with agent count growing faster.
8. US activity is priced in USD at the fixed planning rate £1 = $1.27 (COMPANY.md §1) and starts in Q1 FY2027 when the NYC office opens.
9. Event spend is committed mostly 3 to 6 months ahead; 30% of event spend is assumed non-refundable once committed.
10. Marketing spend that supports brand awareness (awards, analyst relations, brand and web) is included in CAC without attribution to leads, which makes the CAC figures conservative.
11. Marketing does not carry cost of revenue; it is an operating expense (COMPANY.md §5.1: only `delivery` and `customer_success` are cost of revenue).

### Sensitivities (FY2027 unless stated)

| Change | Effect | Response |
|---|---|---|
| Win rate 25% instead of 30% | New logos 37.5 not 45; blended CAC £105.3k not £87.8k (+£17.5k, +20%) | Raise proposal quality; do not expand programme spend to compensate |
| Win rate 35% | New logos 52.5; blended CAC £75.2k (-14%) | Lower lead requirement; hold spend |
| Lead-to-qualified 20% instead of 25% | 300 opportunities need 1,500 leads: +300 leads x £1,250 programme CPL = +£375k (+20% of FY2027 budget) | Tighten ICP and lead scoring before adding spend |
| Paid media CPL +20% | Holding lead volume costs +£54k (+3% of budget) | Rebalance toward content and partner channels |
| Event cancellations (venue or speaker failure) | Exposure £96k (30% of £320k non-refundable) | Cancellation clauses; contingency reallocation to roundtables |
| Marketing-sourced share 50% instead of 60% | Marketing cost per marketing-sourced logo +20%; Sales must source more | Joint pipeline review with the CRO |
| Marketing-sourced deals skew to Spark and Pilot (average first-year value 30% below ACV) | Revenue per £1 of spend falls 4.4x to 3.1x; gross-margin return 2.5x to 1.8x | Nurture to Build and Keep; measure on Keep attach |
| Hiring delayed one quarter (Content Lead, Events and Marketing Operations specialists) | People saving about £72k; content and event capacity falls 15% to 20% | Use freelancers from the content line; no net saving after cover |
| USD strengthens 5% | FY2027 US spend about £120k: +£6k; FY2028 US spend about £470k: +£23.5k | Quote US vendors in USD with a fixed rate where possible |
| AI unit costs fall 15% as planned | FY2027 AI line £60k becomes about £51k at constant usage | Reinvest in more agent seats |
| FY2028 spend outpaces revenue | Marketing is 9.7% of revenue in FY2028 (£4.5m / £46.2m), 9.5% in FY2027 and 13.8% in FY2026 | Stage-gate the FY2028 paid and US lines on the FY2027 CAC |
